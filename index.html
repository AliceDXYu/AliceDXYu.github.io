About

Population science with a molecular lens

My research integrates nutritional epidemiology, metabolomics, metagenomics, clinical data, and longitudinal cohorts to study how diet and the gut microbiome shape cardiometabolic health. The goal is to identify modifiable pathways, predictive biomarkers, and intervention targets that can improve chronic-disease prevention and treatment.

Research

From exposures to mechanisms to health outcomes

01

Nutritional epidemiology

Dietary patterns and food exposures across demographically and geographically diverse populations, with outcomes spanning cardiovascular disease, diabetes, dementia, liver disease, and cancer.

02

Multi-omics

Metabolomics and metagenomics to characterize diet–microbiota–host interactions and discover biomarkers relevant to disease prevention and precision medicine.

03

Gut microbiome

Microbial species, pathways, and metabolites as mediators linking diet, obesity treatment, cardiometabolic health, and chronic-disease risk.

04

EHR & clinical epidemiology

Electronic health record–based analyses and prospective studies of health trajectories and longitudinal multi-omics after obesity treatment.

Approach

A rigorous path from population evidence to translation

Studies are designed to connect interpretable epidemiologic questions with complementary data sources, replication, and biological context.

  1. Define the causal or predictive question
  2. Measure exposures, omics, and outcomes
  3. Analyze diverse cohorts and clinical data
  4. Validate findings across populations
  5. Translate evidence into prevention targets

Selected programs

Research across cohorts and clinical settings

  • Coronary heart disease metabolomics

    Prospective, multi-ethnic, multi-stage studies of gut microbial metabolites, cardiovascular risk, and risk prediction.

  • Gut Microbiota in Metabolic Surgery (GUMMY)

    A multi-ethnic, longitudinal study integrating repeated biospecimens, surveys, electronic health records, and multi-omics after bariatric surgery.

  • Collaborative cohort science

    Population-based research spanning diverse US and international cohorts, with collaboration across epidemiology, surgery, biostatistics, informatics, cardiology, and endocrinology.
